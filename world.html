Our civilizational horizon

CORE exists to accelerate human civilization by turning the world's problems into evidence, evidence into solutions, and solutions into measurable progress.

Long-term ambition: an evolving model of Earth’s energy, water, infrastructure and knowledge. Kardashev Types I, II and III frame the horizon, not a capability or promised outcome.

    What works today

    Working (in this browser)

    • Interactive 3D Earth; dated public data for one area: the Folsom / Howard corridor in San Francisco.
    • Recorded problems with their evidence, sources and declared gaps.
    • Software agents (generated identities, not people) and their missions; questions answered only from recorded data.
    • Contributions (observations, ideas): private by default, kept in this browser; local pseudonymous profile, no account.
    • Recorded traffic simulations (SUMO; synthetic demand, uncalibrated) and a mission report.

    Experimental

    • Live AI investigation: only with a local model on your own computer; the public site replays a recorded run.
    • Expert network, communities and debate arena: fictional people.

    Long-term vision (not built yet)

    • Accounts and contributions shared between people.
    • Coverage beyond one street; verified real-world impact (none so far).

    What you see

    • Real geography: OpenStreetMap streets and building footprints; USGS relief; NASA imagery at planet scale.
    • Nearby buildings: real shapes and heights, procedural façades (a visual convention, not photographs).
    • Trees: real positions from the DataSF inventory; approximate size; generic shape.
    • Markers: problems recorded by CORE, coloured by level of evidence.
    • Agents (CORE World and Hybrid View): software identities from the CORE registry, animated by a deterministic local engine; holographic figures, never people; drawn on mapped walkways; movement is accelerated.
    • Vehicles: only while replaying a recorded SUMO simulation (synthetic demand, uncalibrated model).
